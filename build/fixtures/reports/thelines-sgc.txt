Name of company Sportscard Guaranty Corporation (SGC) Year of establishment 1998 Purpose To inspect, authenticate, and assign a numerical condition grade (1 to 10) to sports trading cards and trading card games Gradin criteria Centering, corners, edges, and surface Scale 1 to 10 scale (including half-grades) Highest grade given to a trading card 10 (Pristine) Turnaround tines Expedited (for card values of at least $3,500): 2 to 3 business days Standard (for card values up to $3,500): 40+ business days Grading costs $50 (standard) for cards worth up to $1,500 Up to $2,000 (expedited) for cards worth up to $100,000 Let’s have a closer look at SGC SGC is one of the old names in the trading card grading business. It opened its doors in 1998 and has built a reputation for bringing vintage cards, which still holds true 28 years later. But there’s more to its history. In 2024, Collectors, the parent company of PSA (Professional Sports Authenticator grading), acquired SGC. That meant that these two grading companies share a corporate owner but chose to grade as separate companies. We’ll touch more on this later on this page. As we noted in our TAG card grading review , this company carefully inspects whether a trading card is real, rates its condition, grades it, and seals it in a hard plastic case called a slab. Every slab gets a certification number. If you input that number on the SGC website, you can confirm the trading card is real. This is especially useful when you’re buying a graded card from someone else or a third-party marketplace. Once the grading is done, the card is sealed in a tamper-proof plastic case called a slab. It’s often called the tuxedo slab because it looks sharp, with a sleek black insert border paired with a minimalist black-and-white header label. The high-contrast black border frames the card cleanly, making the center, edges, and corners stand out. The outer shell is chemically welded using ultrasonic sealing. This prevents tampering or unauthorized opening. How SGC grades trading cards SGC inspects, authenticates, and assigns a numerical condition grade to trading cards brought to its desk. It uses a 1 to 10 scale with half grades (1.5, 2.5, …, 8.5, and 9.5). This grading system is similar to what we found during our PSA card grading review . That said, here’s a breakdown of the top grades on the 10-point scale used by Sportscard Guaranty Corporation: Scale number What it means Pristine 10 A perfect card, shown with a gold label Gem Mint 10 Almost perfect, with only tiny flaws Mint+ 9.5 and Mint 9 Looks sharp, with small signs of wear Near Mint (NM) - Mint Plus (MT+) 8.5 and lower Wear gets easier to see as the number drops Based on the information above, the higher the scale number, the more valuable the trading card becomes. To provide an accurate grade, SGC evaluates 4 sub-grade pillars: centering, corners, edges, and surface. Here’s what we mean: Centering This criterion measures how evenly the printed image is aligned within the card’s borders on both the front and back. Corners This examines each corner’s sharpness, shape, and wear under magnification. Edges This assesses the card’s edges for smoothness, chip-free borders, notching, or fraying. Surface This criterion checks whether the front and back of the card are free of scratches, dents, print lines, loss of gloss, or foil flaws. After evaluation, SGC assigns an overall grade (1-10). That differs from the grading system we found in our BGS card grading review . Beckett Grading Services (BGS) provides 4 distinct sub-grades on its label (Centering, Corners, Edges, and Surface), which are used to calculate an overall final grade. How much does SGC grading cost right now? It depends on the fair market value of the trading card you bring to SGC. This is where SGC pricing changes the most. Previously, the cheapest tier was $15 per card. Now, SGC charges a standard $50 fee on cards with a $1,500 value. For cards valued between $1,500 and $3,500, grading costs fall into two tiers: a $85 standard fee or a $150 expedited fee. At this declared value, SGC lets you choose either standard or expedited grading. For cards that are worth $3,500 or more, SGC switches to expedited grading, which has a faster turnaround time (2 to 3 business days). This fee continues to climb, reaching $2,000 for cards with fair market value of up to $100,000. After that, SGC adds $375 for every additional $10,000 of value. So make sure the card is worth it before you send it to SGC for grading and sealing. How long does it take for SGC to grade your card? According to SGC’s official homepage, standard grading orders ($3,500 and below) take 40+ business days. The plus (+) sign simply means that if orders are high, the process can take longer. So, it can reach 60 days (~2 months) or more. It’s important to note that the turnaround clock starts after SGC logs your cards into its system for grading, not the day you ship them or when your package arrives at its desk. Here’s a scenario to help you understand the turnaround time. Let’s say you want to grade and seal some of your basketball vintage trading cards. You decided to ship your cards in a package to SGC on October 1, and they arrived on October 5. Then it took SGC an extra 3 days, until October 8, to log each of your cards. Your countdown begins on October 8. All things being equal, you should get your card back around November 18, which marks the end of the 40-day timeline. But as explained earlier, the standard grading process can take up to 60 days (about December 8). Interestingly, the expedited grading process takes around 3 days, and it’s for trading cards worth at least $3,500. This duration matches the turnaround time we found for Luxury-tier cards in our recent ACE card grading review . But you’ll need to pay SGC a lot more for that speed. As explained earlier, if your trading card is worth up to $3,500, you can pay $85 for standard grading or $150 for expedited grading. Who should use SGC? SGC is a great fit if you collect vintage sports cards or love the tuxedo slab. It also works well for cards in the mid-range, around $3,500 to $10,000 in value. However if you're more interested in Pokemon cards then you should take a look at our guide on how to grade Pokemon cards which provides more details in this area. If you want a faster turnaround, SGC offers grading services, whereby you only need to wait about 3 business days. But you’ll have to pay more for such service. If you’re not in a rush, the standard turnaround of 40+ business days works fine for a raw card. That said, it may not be worth it for lower-tier trading cards. With a base $50 fee plus shipping and insurance, these costs can quickly eat up your card’s market value. So before you send your item over, always run the following quick checks: Value: Is the card worth more than the grading fee? Goal: Do you want to sell the card or protect a card you love? Condition: Does it look clean with sharp corners and even borders to receive a strong grade number? Overall, SGC is a good option if you have vintage cards you want to grade. For modern cards, buyers often pay more for PSA slabs because they resell better. So if you want to resell your SGC-graded card, the price offer could be lower. But if you plan to keep your card, the slab’s look and protection are worth it. Smart tips to keep in mind before submitting your trading card to SGC Before you submit to SGC’s grading process, please consider these 2 tips to save you money and stress. Sort your cards first Go through your whole stack and send only the cards that are worth the fee. To illustrate, you wouldn’t pay a mechanic $500 to fix a bike you could replace for $80. The same logic applies here. If the grading, shipping, and insurance fees add up to more than the card could gain in value, leave it raw. Check the card yourself Grab a bright light and look closely at three things: centering, corners, and the surface. Tilt the card slowly and look for scratches, print lines, or dents you might miss at a glance. Catching a flaw now helps you decide whether it’s worth submitting.
