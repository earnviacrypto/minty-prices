Official sources: Beckett digital platforms maintenance notice · Beckett card submission services · Beckett Base and Standard service update (historical pre-outage notice) What Sets BGS Apart Why collectors choose Beckett grading Beckett makes the path from Gem Mint to Pristine unusually transparent, especially when you select a service that displays all four subgrades. Our complete BGS grading guide explains the scale, subgrades, and Black Label requirements in detail. Four visible subgrades Centering, Corners, Edges, and Surface each receive a score. The label gives buyers a compact condition report rather than only one overall number. Half-point 1–10 scale BGS uses half-point increments across its 1–10 scale. A 9.5 is Gem Mint, giving strong cards a recognized tier between Mint 9 and the two Pristine 10 outcomes. BGS Black Label requirements A Black Label Pristine 10 requires a perfect 10 in Centering, Corners, Edges, and Surface. Beckett&#x27;s current Pristine standard calls for 50/50 front centering, 55/45 or better on the back, and virtually flawless corners, edges, and surface under close inspection. Meeting a centering ratio alone does not qualify a card for Black Label. Choose the service, not a shortcut A higher-priced Beckett tier buys speed, not an easier standard. Base is currently estimated at 75+ business days, while Priority is estimated at 5, and every tier follows the same published grading scale. Beckett still authenticates, physically inspects, and assigns the official grade. Physical card review Inspect the front and reverse under diffuse and angled light. Check every corner and edge for whitening, compression, chipping, or dents. Do not clean, trim, press, recolor, or otherwise alter the card. A photo cannot reveal every defect or authentication issue, so inspect the physical card too. Image-based pre-screen Use sharp, glare-controlled images with the full card visible. Review the returned centering, corner, edge, and surface observations. Recheck each flagged area on the physical card before deciding what to send. A pre-screen is an inspection aid—not authentication, appraisal, or a guaranteed BGS outcome. What does BGS stand for, and what does the service do? BGS stands for Beckett Grading Services, Beckett&#x27;s professional card authentication, grading, and encapsulation business. Collectors commonly use “BGS” and “Beckett grading” interchangeably. Do Beckett grading services include subgrades? Beckett displays Centering, Corners, Edges, and Surface subgrades on services that include them. Beckett also lists a lower-priced Base option without subgrades when that tier is available. Review the selected service because availability and inclusions can change. Should I pre-screen cards before sending them to Beckett? An image-based pre-screen can help organize visible centering, corner, edge, and surface observations before you pay and ship.
